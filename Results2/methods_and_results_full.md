================================================================================
GEOMETRIC ASSESSMENT OF A FUSED PHOTOGRAMMETRIC / OPEN-DATA CAMPUS MODEL
Full methods, formulae and results
================================================================================

Site         : Otto von Guericke University campus, Magdeburg, Germany
Engine       : Unity 6000.1.5f1, Cesium for Unity
Reference    : 14 RTK-GNSS ground control points, ETRS89 / UTM zone 32N
Survey date  : 2024-12-01

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. REFERENCE DATA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fourteen ground control points were observed with network-RTK (VRS) GNSS in
ETRS89 / UTM zone 32N. Each point was averaged over 201 epochs with a fixed
integer ambiguity solution. Reported coordinate precision:

    horizontal RMS : 0.010 - 0.014 m
    vertical   RMS : 0.011 - 0.025 m
    PDOP           : 1.3 - 3.4

This is roughly one order of magnitude better than the expected accuracy of the
models under test, which justifies treating the GNSS coordinates as reference
("truth") rather than as a second measurement of comparable quality.

GCP 1 defines the origin of the Cesium georeference:

    georeference origin : 52.13958956 N, 11.64030482 E, h = 96.2461 m
    GCP 1               : 52.13958961 N, 11.64030469 E, h = 96.240  m

Agreement is at the centimetre level, so GCP 1 is the datum and not an
independent observation. It is excluded from the photogrammetric accuracy
statistics, leaving n = 13 checkpoints.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. COORDINATE FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Geodetic to Earth-Centred Earth-Fixed (ECEF)

For a point with geodetic latitude phi, longitude lambda and ellipsoidal
height h on the WGS84 ellipsoid:

    a  = 6 378 137.0 m                (semi-major axis)
    f  = 1 / 298.257223563            (flattening)
    e2 = 2f - f^2                     (first eccentricity squared)

    N(phi) = a / sqrt( 1 - e2 * sin^2(phi) )          (prime vertical radius)

    X = ( N(phi) + h ) * cos(phi) * cos(lambda)
    Y = ( N(phi) + h ) * cos(phi) * sin(lambda)
    Z = ( N(phi) * (1 - e2) + h ) * sin(phi)

2.2 ECEF to engine local frame

Cesium for Unity defines a left-handed East-Up-North (EUN) frame tangent to the
ellipsoid at the georeference origin. A point is mapped as

    p_local = R_EUN * ( X_ecef - X_origin )

where R_EUN is the rotation from ECEF to the local tangent basis at the origin
and X_origin is the ECEF position of the georeference. Consequently

    p_local.x  ->  East   (metres)
    p_local.y  ->  Up     (metres)
    p_local.z  ->  North  (metres)

All measurements below are expressed in metres in this frame. Because the frame
is metric and locally Cartesian, residuals in local coordinates are directly
interpretable as metric distances; no further scaling is applied.

2.3 Inverse UTM (used only for terrain tile attribution)

Each DGM tile is named by its south-west corner in UTM zone 32N. Tile corners
were converted to geodetic coordinates using the standard inverse transverse
Mercator series (k0 = 0.9996, lambda_0 = 9 deg E), then to the local frame via
2.1 and 2.2, in order to determine which tile geographically contains each
checkpoint.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MEASUREMENT PROCEDURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All surface interrogation is performed by ray casting against the collision
meshes generated by the tileset renderer. A ray is

    r(t) = o + t * d ,    t in [0, T] ,   |d| = 1

and the reported intersection is the smallest t for which the ray meets a
triangle of the target surface. Only mesh colliders are accepted; the axis
aligned proxy volumes attached to tileset roots are rejected, since they do not
represent surface geometry.

3.1 Vertical residuals (photogrammetry and DGM)

For checkpoint i with local coordinates p_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i), where y_i is the
surveyed height, a vertical ray is cast downward from a height offset H above
the point:

    o = p_i + (0, H, 0) ,    d = (0, -1, 0) ,    H = 60 m

The residual is

    dZ_i = y_model,i - y_i

with y_model,i the height of the intersection. A positive residual means the
modelled surface lies above the surveyed point.

Exactly one photogrammetric intersection was obtained at every checkpoint,
confirming that no vegetation canopy intervened.

Terrain attribution. Each 2 km DGM tile is published as a globally-extending
terrain tileset carrying low-resolution fallback geometry outside its own data
footprint. Four terrain surfaces therefore exist beneath every checkpoint. The
residual is computed exclusively against the tile whose UTM footprint contains
the checkpoint. Taking instead the uppermost of all four surfaces yields
-1.795 m with SD 0.968 m, which characterises the fallback terrain and not the
DGM.

3.2 Horizontal facade offsets (LoD2 vs photogrammetry)

For each checkpoint the nearest LoD2 building is located by casting horizontal
rays over all bearings. A fan of rays is then cast towards that facade:

    heights   : 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 m above the checkpoint
    bearings  : theta_0 + {-8, -4, 0, +4, +8} degrees
    => 25 rays per site

For ray j at site i, let t_LoD2 and t_photo be the ray parameters at first
intersection with the LoD2 and photogrammetric surfaces respectively. The signed
facade offset is

    delta_ij = t_photo,ij - t_LoD2,ij

A positive value means the photogrammetric surface lies further from the
checkpoint than the LoD2 wall, i.e. the LoD2 wall protrudes toward the observer.

Rays for which |delta_ij| > 15 m are discarded as having intersected different
structures. Since both rays share an origin and direction, delta is the
perpendicular offset between the two surfaces along that direction, and is
insensitive to the position of the ray origin.

Rationale for a plane-based rather than surface-based metric. LoD2 is a
generalised representation: extruded cadastral footprints with idealised roof
forms, without balconies, projections or facade relief. A surface-to-surface
metric (e.g. Hausdorff or cloud-to-mesh distance) would conflate this
level-of-detail difference with positional error. The offset along a shared ray
is dominated by the relative position of the two facade planes and is only
weakly affected by generalisation, so it isolates the quantity of interest.

3.3 Vertical roof differences (LoD2 vs photogrammetry)

Roof samples are taken on a grid stepped inward from the facade:

    inward offsets  : 4, 7, 10, 14 m beyond the facade intersection
    lateral offsets : -6, -3, 0, +3, +6 m perpendicular to the ray
    => up to 20 samples per site

At each sample a vertical ray records the uppermost LoD2 and photogrammetric
intersections, h_LoD2 and h_photo. The roof difference is

    dH_ij = h_LoD2,ij - h_photo,ij

Samples are retained only where both surfaces lie more than 3 m above local
ground, which excludes ground and low-vegetation intersections. Samples with
|dH_ij| > 12 m are discarded as gross mismatches.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. STATISTICAL TREATMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Two-level aggregation

Rays within a site are not independent observations: they interrogate the same
pair of surfaces and share systematic error. Pooling all rays would constitute
pseudo-replication and would allow a single noisy site to dominate the result.

A robust site statistic is therefore formed first,

    delta_tilde_i = median_j ( delta_ij )

and summary statistics are computed across the m site values. The median is used
at site level because photogrammetric outliers are strongly one-sided
(vegetation and clutter occlude the facade, never recede behind it).

4.2 Summary statistics

For site values x_1 ... x_m:

    mean          mu   = (1/m) * sum_i x_i
    sample SD     s    = sqrt( (1/(m-1)) * sum_i (x_i - mu)^2 )
    RMSE          rmse = sqrt( (1/m) * sum_i x_i^2 )

RMSE is taken about zero, not about the mean: it is an accuracy measure
expressing total departure from the reference. Bias and dispersion combine as

    rmse^2 = mu^2 + ((m-1)/m) * s^2

Worked check for the photogrammetric vertical residuals (m = 13):

    mu  = 0.055 ,  s = 0.094
    mu^2 = 0.003025
    (12/13) * 0.094^2 = 0.008156
    sqrt(0.003025 + 0.008156) = 0.1057  ->  0.106 m   (matches the reported RMSE)

Separating mu from s is diagnostically important: a non-zero mu indicates a
systematic datum or reference offset, correctable by translation, whereas s
reflects random reconstruction error and generalisation, which is not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. INTERPRETATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Horizontal. LoD2 facades agree with the photogrammetric facades to
RMSE 0.167 m with a mean offset of 0.004 m. The bias is two orders of magnitude
below the dispersion, so no systematic planimetric shift is detectable between
the datasets. The residual dispersion of 0.175 m is consistent with the combined
effect of LoD2 generalisation and photogrammetric surface noise, and lies well
within the positional accuracy specified for LoD2 building models derived from
German cadastral data (approximately 0.5 m).

Vertical. Both open datasets sit systematically below the RTK reference:
-3.281 m for the terrain model and -3.678 m for LoD2 roofs. The terrain offset
has a standard deviation of only 0.028 m across fourteen points, i.e. it is a
constant translation rather than a distribution of errors. That two
independently produced products from the same national open-data infrastructure
exhibit offsets of the same order indicates a common vertical reference
inconsistency in the ingestion path rather than a fault in either dataset.

The offset is smaller than the geoid undulation for this region (approximately
45 m), so it does not correspond to a simple orthometric-to-ellipsoidal
confusion; its origin is not established here.

Consequence. Lateral extension of the environment beyond the photogrammetric
footprint using open CityGML and DGM data is justified: horizontal agreement is
unbiased at the decimetre level, which is the condition governing whether
geometry lines up across the boundary. Vertical continuity requires an explicit
correction of approximately 3.3 m applied to both open datasets. Because the
correction is a constant translation, applying it does not disturb the internal
geometry of either dataset.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Facade measurements assess horizontal offset perpendicular to building walls;
  roof measurements assess vertical offset only. Neither addresses rotational
  misalignment, which would require feature correspondence rather than surface
  sampling.

- At GCP 5 and 7 the facade and roof measurements were taken on different
  structures, because the closest surface at those sites is a low structure
  without a usable roof. Those two rows do not describe the same building.

- The cause of the vertical offset is not established and is identified as
  future work.

- Whether the GCPs were used as control during photogrammetric bundle adjustment
  or withheld as independent checkpoints determines whether the photogrammetric
  vertical figures represent independent accuracy or fitting residuals.
  [STATE WHICH APPLIES.]

- Measurements were made with the LoD2 clipping mask disabled so that both
  datasets coexisted in the overlap region; the deployed configuration
  suppresses LoD2 geometry inside the photogrammetric footprint.
